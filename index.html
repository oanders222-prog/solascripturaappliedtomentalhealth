What the Bible Says About Mental Health

Anxiety, fear, guilt, peace, self-control, discipleship, and the love of God

A Biblical Way to Think About the Mental Health Crisis

We are currently in a mental health crisis, with the numbers going up and up. Many college students report anxiety and depression. There are medical and genetic aspects to this but there is also certaintly a sin and fallenness aspect to it. We are also in a time when faithfulness to the Bible decreases more and more. The Bible speaks to this problem—not by pretending suffering is simple, but by teaching us what is wrong with the world, what is wrong in us, and where peace is found through restored communion with God.

  1. We are born into a fallen world under the curse of God. Things are not right. The world contains suffering, loss, disappointment, disease, conflict, and death.
  2. Our own hearts are in rebellion against God. We minimize our own sin while fretting about why the world is not as we want it to be. Scripture teaches us to examine our own hearts, confess sin, and seek forgiveness.
  3. The Bible teaches self-control and self-mastery. We are not to be tossed about by every feeling, fear, or doubt that comes along. Feelings are real, but they are not our master. Our minds and desires are to be formed by the truth of God.
  4. To grow in this, we must be disciples of Christ. We should expect a daily struggle throughout our lives. We can rejoice when we reach a peak, while knowing there is another climb ahead. Christian maturity is not the absence of struggle; it is learning to struggle faithfully.
  5. God disciplines those he loves. The very fact that there is a spiritual battle within the Christian is evidence of God’s redemptive work in our lives. He does not abandon his children. He trains, corrects, strengthens, and loves them.

The Biblical Movement

1

The Condition of Sin

Scripture shows that rebellion, rejection of God's Word, and sinful habits results in loss of communion with God.

2

The Consequence of Sin

Sin hides, accuses, troubles the heart, and makes us afraid. A conscience at war with God cannot create lasting peace for itself.

3

The Cure of Sin

The biblical answer is not mere self-soothing. It is repentance, forgiveness, faith, communion with Christ, and trust in God’s providence. God freely offers us peace.
